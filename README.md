## About the Project

This repository contains the project management plan for the Rocky Mountain Rail Authority (RMRA) High-Speed Rail Feasibility Study in Colorado. The feasibility study evaluates whether high-speed intercity passenger rail can be implemented along the I-25 Front Range corridor, the I-70 corridor, and selected secondary corridors serving mountain communities and tourism destinations.

The analysis combines transportation planning, engineering, ridership forecasting, operations planning, financial analysis, economic evaluation, stakeholder engagement, and implementation planning. The goal is to provide RMRA and its partners with an objective basis for deciding whether to advance high-speed rail planning and pursue future federal funding or corridor designation.

This is an academic project completed by Group 1 for the Department of Management at California State University, East Bay.

## Objectives

- Determine the technical and economic feasibility of high-speed rail in the I-25 and I-70 corridors.
- Evaluate service and technology options capable of sustained speeds of at least 90 mph.
- Forecast ridership, revenue, capital costs, operating costs, and financial performance.
- Assess travel-time, reliability, congestion, community, economic, and societal benefits.
- Recommend preferred alternatives and define the next steps for implementation.

## Scope

### In Scope

- Project governance, coordination, reporting, quality control, and document control.
- Work breakdown structure, schedule, budget, staffing, communications, and change control.
- Stakeholder engagement, public outreach, policy workshops, and scoping.
- Market, engineering, technology, property, and station databases.
- Service concepts, route and technology alternatives, station strategies, and alignment analysis.
- Ridership, revenue, operating-cost, capital-cost, financial, and economic analyses.
- Final feasibility, business, and implementation documentation.

### Out of Scope

- Detailed engineering design.
- Right-of-way acquisition.
- Environmental Impact Statement preparation.
- Final construction, procurement, or implementation commitments.

The study provides the analytical and strategic foundation for later phases; it does not commit RMRA to construction or implementation.

## Major Deliverables

| Deliverable | Purpose |

| Stakeholder Engagement & Scoping | Define transportation needs, collect public input, and build stakeholder alignment. |

## Project Timeline

- **Baseline start:** June 1, 2008
- **Baseline finish:** July 14, 2009
- **Total duration:** 293 days
- **Primary critical path:** Project management → peer review → scoping → methodology and databases → alternatives analysis → feasibility determination → final documentation

The methodology and database work is the longest technical block. Alternatives analysis cannot begin until the baseline data and methodology are sufficiently complete, making delays in these activities especially consequential.

## Budget and Cost Approach

The project uses bottom-up labor estimates where task-level information is available, with analogous or apportionment estimates for smaller or similar activities.

### Cost Formula

```text
Direct labor = sum of role hours x hourly rate
Indirect cost = 30% of direct labor
Contingency = 15% of direct labor plus indirect cost
Total cost = direct labor + indirect cost + contingency
```

The report establishes a baseline Budget at Completion (BAC) of **$595,010**, consisting of:

- Direct labor: $398,000
- Indirect costs: $119,400
- Contingency: $77,610

The report also presents a broader resource summary of **$1,249,763**, including a labor budget of $1,100,663 and an expense budget of $149,100. These figures should be interpreted as a broader resource-plan view, while $595,010 is the stated control-account cost baseline.

## Risk Management

Risks are assessed using a probability-impact score:

```text
Risk score = probability x impact
```

The highest-priority risks include:

- Stakeholder resistance to proposed rail alignments.
- Delays in data collection from partner agencies.
- Budget growth caused by scope expansion.
- Technical complexity in the I-70 mountain corridor.
- Ridership assumptions being challenged during external or FRA review.

Risks are reviewed weekly by the project manager and risk owners, updated monthly with the full team, and reported monthly to the Steering Committee. Critical risks are escalated to the Steering Committee or RMRA Board.

## Change Management: Underground DIA Station

During stakeholder review, the team received a request to evaluate an underground station at Denver International Airport instead of the originally assumed above-ground station.

The change was handled through the project change-control process:

1. Document the requested change.
2. Assess impacts on scope, schedule, cost, resources, engineering, and stakeholders.
3. Update affected WBS work packages, dependencies, databases, and analysis assumptions.
4. Coordinate with airport facilities, engineering, infrastructure, and safety stakeholders.
5. Communicate the approved change to the project team.
6. Monitor cost, schedule, risk, and quality impacts through completion.

The estimated incremental cost of the change was **$18,113**. The report concludes that the added work could be absorbed without changing the baseline schedule or requiring a full re-baseline. The change strengthened the study by making the airport option more forward-looking and realistic.

## Success Criteria

The project is considered successful when it:

- Delivers a complete, credible study on schedule and within the approved cost baseline.
- Identifies at least one preferred corridor option that meets the required operating and cost-benefit criteria.
- Produces defensible ridership and revenue forecasts that can support external review and decision-making.
- Documents stakeholder and public input and builds regional support around preferred alternatives.
- Produces approved feasibility, business, and implementation plans with prior review comments resolved.

## Project Controls

The project uses the following controls:

- Version-controlled project documentation.
- WBS/OBS integration and control accounts.
- Time-phased resource planning.
- Schedule-network and critical-path analysis.
- Monthly scope, schedule, cost, and risk reporting.
- Peer reviews and quality checks for major technical outputs.
- A formal change log with impact assessment and approval records.
